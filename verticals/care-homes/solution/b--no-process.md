## b) Facilities with no Process

A facility with no reliable process may already have forms, messages and registers, but staff depend on memory and individual initiative. Do not start by buying software or moving every record. First observe the real work, agree on a safe minimum process and introduce one controlled workflow at a time.

---

## Describe the Situation

Requests may arrive by conversation, phone, messaging apps or paper. There may be no consistent owner, status, due date or handover, and patients or carers may not know whether their request reached the right team.

| Area | Common current condition |
| Records | Several informal sources, no agreed source of truth |
| Teams | Work depends on personal relationships and memory |
| Technology | Personal email or messaging may be mixed with official work |
| Public website | May be absent, outdated or disconnected from real service procedures |
| Migration goal | Define and test the process before automating it |

---

## Implementation Challenges

| Common challenge | Systems engineer can add value | DAWN process team can add value |
| 1. Requests arrive through many channels | Inventory channels and provide a single approved intake route where feasible | Agree which channels are accepted and how each is triaged |
| 2. No one owns open requests | Create a minimal owner/status register in an approved tool | Define accountable roles, deputies and acceptance expectations |
| 3. Staff cannot see what is overdue | Add simple status, date and due-time views | Set a realistic review rhythm and escalation practice |
| 4. Handover relies on memory | Create a clear paper or approved digital handover list | Coach outgoing/incoming teams on explicit transfer of responsibility |
| 5. Duplicate or conflicting records | Identify the authoritative record for each information type | Agree how duplicate entries are reconciled and corrected |
| 6. Patients/carers receive no acknowledgement | Configure approved acknowledgement templates and routing | Set service expectations for acknowledgement and updates |
| 7. Sensitive details are shared informally | Provide safer channels and remove confidential fields from general trackers | Train staff in minimum disclosure and recipient verification |
| 8. Escalations are inconsistent | Make priority and escalation paths visible in internal guidance | Define urgency criteria with clinical and safeguarding leads |
| 9. Staff see change as extra paperwork | Simplify forms and avoid re-entering data | Pilot with frontline users and remove steps that add no value |
| 10. Management lacks a baseline | Produce aggregate, non-identifying measures from the workflow | Review delays and failure points without blaming individuals |

---

## Worksflows in Migration

Map actual work before designing the target state. Pilot one common request type, such as a facilities issue or routine appointment coordination, while urgent and clinical matters continue to use existing formal escalation routes.

| Step | Workflow | Required check |
| 1 | Observe how requests currently enter and move | Include every shift and relevant team |
| 2 | Agree intake, triage, assignment, update and closure definitions | Clinical and safeguarding cases have separate approved routes |
| 3 | Select a simple approved tracker and paper fallback | Exclude unnecessary patient identifiers and clinical detail |
| 4 | Write short email/message templates and internal instructions | Verify recipient, authority and channel before any personal update |
| 5 | Run a time-limited pilot with one team | Compare requests received with requests assigned and completed |
| 6 | Revise and expand only after staff confirm the process works | Keep a documented change history |

## Workflows and Operating Procedures

Use the Teams proposal’s structured templates, task owners, statuses and links as a process pattern. A tracker should contain only the operational information required to coordinate work; it should point to the authoritative record rather than become a second case file.

| State or procedure | Operating rule |
| Received | Date, classify and acknowledge through the approved route |
| Triaged | Identify urgency; immediately route emergencies and safeguarding issues |
| Assigned | Name the accountable team/owner and next review time |
| Waiting | Record the dependency and who will follow up |
| Resolved | Enter the result in the correct authoritative record |
| Closed | Confirm that the action and any required notification are complete |

---

## Medical Related Workflows

Do not treat an unstructured request tracker as a clinical record or triage tool. Clinical prioritization, diagnosis, treatment, medication, consent and discharge decisions belong to qualified staff using approved procedures.

| Workflow | Minimum control |
| Incoming symptom or deterioration report | Escalate promptly to the designated clinical or emergency pathway |
| Clinical documentation | Record in the authorized patient record, not general email or work tracker |
| Team coordination | Share a limited notification with the responsible clinical team and link/reference only if access is approved |
| Patient/carer communication | A designated staff member confirms identity and authorization before disclosure |
| Missed follow-up | Escalate to the clinical lead according to local policy and document the action taken |
