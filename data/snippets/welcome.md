## 2. The four-step Prometheus product

Prometheus guides organisations through four connected stages:

1. **Marketing:** make the mission clear and discoverable through an owned digital home.
2. **Federation:** connect independent organisations and resources while preserving local identity and governance.
3. **Security:** reduce unnecessary exposure through thoughtful architecture, proportionate safeguards, and sound operating practices.
4. **CRM / ERP:** connect enquiries and relationships to agreements, projects, delivery, and operational reporting.

These are not isolated services. They form a path from public understanding to connected, secure operations. Each organisation can begin with what it needs and grow the system as its requirements develop.

Explore [The Prometheus Product](%url%product/) for the detailed outline.

## 3. A world of possibilities with AmadeusWeb Spring

AmadeusWeb Spring is the technology foundation for Prometheus and DAWN. Its file-based approach, familiar web formats, folder-derived navigation, local editing, Git workflow, and flexible publishing options provide a rich set of building blocks for digital homes and content-led workflows.

I use Spring as a foundation, not as a claim that one framework already contains every specialised business application. I begin with the actual requirement and determine whether it is best met through Spring, an extension, an integration, or a specialist product. The aim is to build a coherent solution without adding needless complexity.

Read [A World of Possibilities](%url%2026/2026-10/001--a-world-of-possibilities/) to see how this platform supports the Prometheus vision and how capabilities can develop over time.

## 4. Engineering sense: mission, architecture, and delivery

Good engineering is more than choosing a language or framework. It is the judgment to understand the problem, make deliberate trade-offs, build what is needed, and leave people able to maintain and improve the result.

I bring enterprise experience without assuming every organisation needs enterprise-scale complexity. I use simpler approaches where they are sufficient, robust integrations where they are necessary, and explain the boundaries and responsibilities involved — especially around security, federation, and operational data.
